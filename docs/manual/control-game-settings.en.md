# Guide to running a game

## Control panel

Open the Control Panel from **My games** using `Play`. It is for running a live game; you do not edit questions or poll results here.

The panel guides the operator step by step: connect devices, check game settings, then run the rounds and optional final. Each stage becomes available when the preceding stage is ready, reducing the risk of mistakes during play.

### Before you start

- Prepare questions and answers in the editor. A poll-based game must be finished and approved.
- The operator needs a computer with a screen large enough to read questions, answers and hints comfortably.
- Prepare separate devices for the Display (TV/projector), Host and Buzzer.
- Use stable Wi-Fi. Browser suspension and switching networks are common causes of connection problems.

**Why these preparations?**

A live game moves at television pace. The panel handles the procedure without adding pressure for the operator, so devices and settings must be ready before starting.

### Who sees what

The screens have separate roles:

- **Operator (Control Panel)** — all controls, game status, bank, strikes (X), messages and procedural steps. The operator controls what appears on the board.
- **Display** — questions, answers, points, bank, strikes and opening/ending screens, visible to contestants and the audience.
- **Host** — reading material and context, without game controls. The operator runs the game.
- **Buzzer** — signals which team pressed first in the duel.

The Host tablet has two sections: top/bottom in portrait and left/right in landscape. The first shows the game stage and, when appropriate, the question. The second contains the answer list, final questions or current answer-matching details. This second section can be covered.

**Cover on the Host tablet**

- In portrait, swipe **down** to reveal and **up** to cover.
- In landscape, swipe **right** to reveal and **left** to cover.
- Instructions on the cover and below the revealed material follow the device orientation.
- Revealing is only for the Host: it does not show an answer to the audience or confirm a game action. The cover keeps contestants from accidentally seeing questions and answers.
- **Each new game-state change restores the cover** when that stage requires it. Another swipe may be needed after an operator action.

Reveal the material when you need to read a question or check answers. The cover does not mean that answers have disappeared from the game.

#### Control Panel layout

The top bar shows Display, Host and Buzzer connections. Device buttons let you reopen links, codes and sharing during play. The main area follows the current stage: devices or the Summary during preparation, questions and answers in rounds, and entry fields or individual answer assessment in the final.

The hints column explains who is answering and what action is needed. The bar below the board shows the playing team and bank in rounds, or the final total during final reveals. The next-action button describes its destination: at major transitions, `Next` becomes, for example, `Go to the final` or `Finish final`.

### 1) Devices

Connect devices first. The top bar shows **Display**, **Host** and **Buzzer** status. Connect all required devices; the Host is optional and the game can be hosted directly from the Control Panel.

#### Display

The panel provides a **6-digit code**. Open the Display on a TV or projector in fullscreen without browser bars. The panel allows the next step once the Display is connected.

**Using a second screen**

For best results, extend your desktop: **Windows** uses `Win + P`; on a **Mac**, use `⌘ F1` (sometimes with `Fn`).

Open the board in a separate browser window, move it to the second screen and enable fullscreen. This lets you operate the panel while the audience sees the board.

**TV as a separate device**

On your computer, open the Display code in the Control Panel. Visit www.familiada.online in the TV browser. A recognised TV opens a simplified connection page with Familiada branding, a six-digit code field and `Connect`. Enter the Display code. No TV login or cable connection to the computer is needed.

The code field is focused automatically. Up/down moves between the field and button; OK or Enter confirms. Unlock sound on the Display if audio should play from the TV. The browser may require a remote-control press before allowing fullscreen.

You can also open the Display link copied from the panel directly on the TV. If the browser is not recognised, open the [TV connection page](https://www.familiada.online/connect-device/tv/) and enter the code. This also applies to custom browsers on Apple TV. TVs accept game Display or poll QR Display codes; Host and Buzzer codes are rejected.

#### Host and Buzzer

Connect the Host device and Buzzer next. Each has a **6-digit code** and QR code. Two separate phones, or a phone and tablet, are usually convenient.

`QR on display` shows the QR codes on the connected large screen so the team can scan them without copying links.

Use `Share` to send a link by email or share it with your subscribers. Registered recipients find shared devices under `Connect device` ![](manual-assets/phone.svg). Unregistered recipients receive an email link.

**Connecting a device conveniently**

Visit `familiada.online`, select `Connect device` ![](manual-assets/phone.svg) in the top bar and enter the **6-digit code** shown beside the device in the Control Panel.

Alternatively, scan its **QR code** on a mobile device. Installing the web app is recommended: select ![](manual-assets/download.svg) on **My games** for instructions.

**Without a Host tablet**

Select `No host tablet` beside the Host device if the Host has no separate phone/tablet. Host connection controls and code become unavailable, the Host is no longer required to continue, and its top-bar indicator becomes inactive. The operator can host directly from the panel.

**Physical buzzer**

If you use a hardware buzzer connected separately instead of a browser Buzzer, select `Physical buzzer`. A browser Buzzer connection is no longer required; the operator decides who pressed first.

In this mode:

- The duel shows `Team A` and `Team B` buttons.
- Select the team that pressed first; its tile lights up.
- Select `Confirm` to accept the choice and continue.

**Device disconnection**

When a required device stops reporting its presence, after about 6.5 seconds the panel shows a one-time dialog naming disconnected devices and blocks further actions. Check the panel's and devices' internet connections. If they do not recover, reconnect using top-bar buttons. Play unlocks when all required devices return. If Display is the sound source, unlock sound on Display again after it reconnects. Its button appears automatically and the panel stays blocked until you press it; no page reload is needed. Devices skipped during preparation do not block play.

Previously confirmed actions remain saved. An action already sent may still finish saving; the system does not roll back the result. A returning device displays the current state without replaying missed animations. A running timer keeps counting down.

#### Sound

Choose **Control panel** or **Display** as the sound source; only that device plays game audio. For Display audio, press its sound-unlock button; TV OK or Enter also works. Browsers may require a click before permitting playback. Check the TV/speaker volume too.

**Warning:** Choose Display when audio should play on a separate TV or other device running the Display page. With HDMI, keeping Control panel as the source and selecting the TV as the computer's audio output is usually easier; the system often does this automatically. AirPlay sends video and sound from a Mac, iPhone or iPad to Apple TV.

**Shortcut:** M mutes or restores sound immediately, without Enter.

### 2) Settings

**Host:** The tablet shows the logo selected in game settings. If that logo is being edited earlier, it shows the default Familiada logo. When the operator opens the settings Summary, the tablet reloads the selected logo and replaces the temporary default.

After connecting devices, check the settings Summary. Colours, sounds and game parameters can be configured beforehand in **Game settings** and are loaded automatically.

**Shortcuts:** In the Summary, E selects settings editing and Enter opens it. B followed by Enter goes back when available.

#### Team names

Check names in the Summary. The Display stays black here. Names appear after `Done — start the game`, on the screen with `Start game`. Empty Game settings fields use **Team A** and **Team B**.

#### Appearance

- **Colours** — teams, background and dots.
- **Theme** — board appearance.
- **Logo** — shown during play.

#### Sound

- Each category shows the current **variant**, such as Classic or a custom filename.
- Its **volume** slider adjusts that sound.
- ![](manual-assets/play.svg) previews it before play.

Volume changes here apply only to this game session. The next session loads the values saved in **Game settings**.

#### Final

This shows whether the game includes a final.

In **Manual** mode, it shows your five selected final questions. In **Random** mode, it shows the drawn questions; use `Reshuffle` to draw another set before starting. The specified order and manually selected final questions remain unchanged.

#### Rounds: question order

**Order** mode shows the main-round question sequence. **Random** mode shows the drawn round questions; use `Reshuffle` beside them to change their order before starting.

Use `Change settings` whenever needed to open the settings dialog. See [Game settings](#gameSettings) for details.

When everything is ready, select `Done — start the game`.

#### Selecting and confirming

For tiles requiring confirmation, the first click selects and the second confirms. This applies to accepting a buzzer press, revealing round answers, adding X and passing control. Selection alone does not change the score. Grey buttons are unavailable; wait for the panel to unlock them during sound or transitions. The hints column explains the current step.

**Tip:** Selection lets you check the choice before performing it. Points and the board change only after confirmation. Allow an ongoing sound or transition to finish.

**Important:** Letters and digits in an active text field remain text. Shortcuts respect locks, sound and animations and do not act behind an open dialog. Restart has no shortcut.

### 3) Starting the game

After checking the Summary, select `Done — start the game`. The Display becomes active and shows team names without a logo or answer board. The Buzzer is visible but does not yet accept presses.

**Tip:** You can leave the panel at this stage until the actual show starts. The following stages run at the pace of the game.

`Start game` plays the intro and animated logo entrance. After the transition, select `Start` to open the question board and begin the duel.

**Host:** The question becomes available at the duel stage. Do not read the material before contestants are ready.

### 4) Rounds — step by step

Rounds contain questions, answers, points and the round bank. Contestants watch the Display, the Host asks questions and manages the show, and the operator reveals answers, awards points and adds strikes (X).

The Host focuses on contestants and the operator on the panel, keeping play smooth and the board accurate.

**Tip:** One person can host and operate, but must move efficiently between talking to contestants and using the panel.

#### Duel: taking control

Each question begins with a duel between family representatives. The Buzzer lights up immediately when pressed and tells the operator which team was first. Accept that team. Use `Retry` if the press needs repeating. With a physical buzzer, select the team manually.

If the first answer is not the highest-scoring one, the other representative may give a better answer and take control. The panel guides this decision; the Display indicates the team currently playing.

**Host:** `ROUND 1 — BUZZER` means the duel is in progress, including after the operator accepts a press. Reveal the second section to see the question and full answer list with points. The cover prevents contestants reading the question prematurely. The title's round number changes accordingly.

**Shortcuts:** C then Enter confirms the team indicated by the Buzzer. With a physical buzzer, A/B selects the team and Enter confirms.

#### Passing the question

After taking control, the team may **pass the question** to its opponents as a tactical choice. The panel offers this only at the appropriate moment and prevents repeated misuse.

**Host:** When passing control is possible, the first section shows a red, underlined hint. This style applies only to the Host tablet. The operator carries out the team's decision in the panel.

**Shortcut:** P selects passing control; Enter confirms.

**Host:** The pass-control hint is red and underlined.

#### Playing the question: reveals and bank

Once control is decided, the team answers and the operator selects and confirms matching answers. The Display reveals the answer first, then updates the round bank. Revealed tiles turn green and cannot award points again. The bank belongs to the current question; the team score updates at round settlement with the multiplier applied.

Play continues until every answer is revealed or the playing team has three strikes. The third X opens a steal for the opponents. The panel hint identifies the stage and next action.

**Host:** `ROUND 1 — PLAY` means the team is playing after the duel. The first section shows the question openly. The second has full answers and points, including answers still hidden from the audience. `2) Bicycle (24)` is answer 2 worth 24 points. Green means publicly revealed. The Host does not show the bank, strikes or team scores; check the Display or panel.

**Shortcuts:** 1–6 selects an answer; Enter reveals it.

#### Strikes (X) and the 3-second limit

An incorrect answer adds **X**. Three strikes lose control and allow the opponents to steal. The **3-second** answer timer also counts expiry as a strike.

**Why a timer?**

It helps the operator end hesitation promptly, avoid debate and maintain the game's pace.

**Shortcuts:** X selects a strike, T the timer; Enter performs the selected action.

#### Stealing the bank (one answer)

After three strikes before all answers are revealed, the opponents get **one answer**. A correct answer wins the bank; otherwise the playing team keeps it. This resolves the question and round.

**Host:** The title becomes `ROUND 1 — STEAL`. The answer list remains beneath the cover and the question remains open. Check the Display or panel for the stealing team's name.

#### Remaining answers and ending a round

If another round or the final follows and the question is resolved, select `End round`. The bank is awarded to the appropriate team with the multiplier applied. Reveal any remaining answers to the audience; they award no further team points. The board stays visible until the next transition.

**Practical note:**

The panel separates playing a question from ending a round. This avoids accidentally clearing the board before the Host finishes speaking or thanks the contestants.

**Host:** `ROUND 1 — REVEAL` shows the remaining answers after settlement. Each revealed row turns green.

After revealing remaining answers, use the button for the next round or final.

#### Ending rounds and continuing

After each round, scores update and the ending condition from **Additional settings** is checked. It is usually a threshold such as **300**, but you can choose another for your tournament.

With the final **enabled** and the rounds threshold reached, the next-action button leads to the final. To end without a final, select `Go to end of game` to award the bank and show the ending screen. Reaching the threshold alone does not show the result automatically; other round actions are blocked and remaining answers are not revealed.

Running out of round questions before the threshold ends the main game. It does not qualify a team for the final: the threshold must be reached and the final enabled.

**Warning:** Choose enough questions and a suitable threshold to reach the planned final. Enabling a final alone does not guarantee it will start.

**Shortcut:** N selects the current next-action button; Enter activates it.

### 5) Final

Two contestants answer the same **5 questions**. Their points add up towards the configured target, **200** by default. Usually both come from the winning team. With its agreement, you may organise one contestant from each team; the system still awards one shared final to the winners.

#### Preparing the final

Check the five questions in the Summary before play. Select them in Game settings for **Manual** mode, or let **Random** mode draw them. Prepare a waiting area and headphones playing music for contestant 2, who must not hear the first contestant's questions or answers.

`Start` opens the final board. The winning team's score remains visible, the opponent's score area is reserved for the timer, and the final total appears in the top bank.

**Host:** The tablet shows no material during final preparation. It appears when the first round starts.

#### Player 1 — preparation and entry

Make sure contestant 2 waits outside the game. The Host reads the questions in sequence; the operator enters answers beside the corresponding questions. They are not yet assessed or shown to the audience.

Player 1 has **15 seconds**. The timer does not start simply by opening this stage: use its button or **Ctrl + Enter** (**⌘ + Enter** on Mac). The same control stops it. Enter/down moves to the next empty field; up moves to the previous empty field, wrapping after the last question. Click a completed field to edit it. An empty field means no entry, not a final assessment. Each player must start the timer before matching. Early stopping requires text in all five fields; Repeat alone does not fill a field. Natural expiry allows you to continue with missing entries.

**Tip:** After expiry, correct typos or enter an answer you remember while the Host talks to the contestant. Editing remains possible during matching until the answer is revealed.

**Host during entry:**

The first section shows `FINAL ROUND 1` or `FINAL ROUND 2`. Once the timer starts, the title includes the remaining time, such as `FINAL ROUND 1 — COUNTDOWN 12s`. Without a running timer it shows no seconds.

The second section shows five numbered questions with entry status, for example:

```text
1) Name a means of transport. — entered
2) What do you take on holiday? — missing
3) What do you drink in the morning? — repeat
```

| Question status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `entered` | The operator saved non-empty answer text. It does not confirm a match or award points yet. |
| `missing` | The answer field is empty, not necessarily permanently skipped. |
| `repeat` | Player 2's answer was marked as repeating player 1. It scores zero on reveal. |

The Host reads questions and checks which entries are complete. This screen shows statuses, **not the answers currently being typed**. The entered text appears later during matching.

**Shortcuts:** Ctrl+Enter (⌘+Enter on Mac) starts/stops the timer. ↑ / ↓ / Enter moves only among empty fields; click a completed field to edit it.

#### Player 1 — matching and revealing

After answering, proceed to reveals. Compare each entry with the scoring list and choose a matching answer or `Not on the list (0 pts)`. The Display shows your chosen answer; the full entry remains in the panel. For a match, reveal the answer then its points. For a written incorrect answer, reveal the text then zero. Missing answers automatically reveal the answer and zero. Matching alone awards nothing; the total changes when points are revealed.

**Tip:** Before revealing an unlisted written answer, correct any typos. Once revealed, the answer cannot be changed.

**Host during reveals:**

The first section shows, for example, `FINAL — REVEAL (ROUND 2)` and `Question 3: …`. The number identifies the assessed question; round 2 means the second final player.

The second section contains:

1. **`Player 1: …`** — only while matching player 2. The first player's previously selected answer to that question; `—` means no answer.
2. **`Entered: …`** — the operator's current text. Omitted if there is no entry or Repeat is marked.
3. **`Status: …`** — current matching status, as below.
4. **`Answer list:`** — all scoring answers to the question, highest first, with points in brackets.

| Status | Appearance | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `from list` | Green text; the selected list answer is green and struck through. | The entry was assigned to a scoring answer. Strikethrough means selected, **not incorrect or cancelled**. |
| `not on list` | Yellow text. | There is an entry but no scoring match. Keeping this choice reveals the literal text, then zero points. You can change it before reveal. |
| `no answer` | Red text. | The current player has no answer text. |
| `repeat` | Yellow text. | Player 2 repeated player 1's answer; no points are awarded. |

**Final colours describe matching, not Display animation stages.** A green list match does not by itself mean the audience has seen the answer or points. Distinguish the operator's text from the chosen scoring answer, such as “by bike” matched to “Bicycle (24)”.

During player 1 matching, **1–6** chooses a list answer, **W** the written answer and **O** no answer. Player 2 also has **R** for Repeat. Selection takes one click or shortcut. The first Enter reveals the answer; the next reveals points after the transition. Missing answers and repeats automatically show zero. **N then Enter** advances to the next question. Mouse reveals require selection and a second confirmation click.

#### Player 2 — preparation and entry

If the target is not reached, after player 1's fifth question prepare contestant 2. The Host tablet clears. Explain the rules and optionally demonstrate the repeat sound.

`Start` restores player 1's revealed answers on the Display. Contestant 2 turns away to avoid seeing them. The operator sees those answers beside the entry fields to recognise repeats. The timer is **20 seconds**, started as for player 1. The Host sees `FINAL ROUND 2`, a countdown when running, and five questions with entry statuses.

When contestant 2 repeats player 1's answer, mark `Repeat` with one click and ask for another answer. Every click plays the repeat sound, even if already marked. Clicking again does not remove the mark. While time remains, the Host can reread skipped questions, including repeats, so the contestant can improve the answer. Typing new text automatically clears Repeat; merely focusing the field does not.

**Tip:** Questions can be revisited until time runs out. A contestant saying `Next` does not close the question for the remainder of the round.

**Shortcuts:** Ctrl+Enter (⌘+Enter on Mac) starts/stops the timer. ↑ / ↓ / Enter moves only among empty fields; click to edit completed fields. Shift+Enter in an empty player 2 field marks Repeat and plays its sound. Each further press replays the sound; only typing text clears the mark.

#### Player 2 — matching and revealing

Match and reveal as for player 1. In the panel and on the Host tablet, **Player 1** is the earlier selected answer and **Entered** is the operator's current text; their wording may differ. During matching, `Repeat` is only a marker: selecting it does not play the repeat sound. Revealing it behaves like no answer, automatically showing zero and playing the wrong-answer sound. Both players contribute to the same total at the top of the Display.

**Important:** Display answers fit 17 characters in rounds and 11 in the final, including spaces. Only the board shortens longer text; the panel and Host retain it in full. A dot marks a cut word. If the next character is a space or punctuation, no dot is added. When cutting a word, up to two trailing vowels are also removed before the dot. This applies to Polish, English and Ukrainian answers; the dot counts towards the limit.

During player 1 matching, **1–6** chooses a list answer, **W** the written answer and **O** no answer. Player 2 also has **R** for Repeat. Selection takes one click or shortcut. The first Enter reveals the answer; the next reveals points after the transition. Missing answers and repeats automatically show zero. **N then Enter** advances to the next question. Mouse reveals require selection and a second confirmation click.

#### When the final ends

Reaching the target blocks further answer assessment. The hints column reports the target and the button becomes `Finish final`, for either player 1 or 2. The result is not automatic; the operator chooses when to finish. If the target is not reached, `Finish final` appears after player 2's last points are revealed.

`Finish final` shows the configured ending screen: logo, points or prize amount. The following `Finish game` only plays **Show outro music**. The result stays visible and the winning team's indicator remains lit. The Host tablet clears when the final ends; read the result on the Display.

#### Ending without a final

If the final is disabled and the rounds threshold is reached, or questions run out, other round actions are blocked. Do not reveal remaining answers. `Go to end of game` awards the bank and shows the ending screen. Then `Finish game` only plays **Show outro music**. With **Show prize amount (after final)**, ending without a final shows points; a tie shows the logo.

The Host tablet clears when proceeding to the game ending. Read the result on the Display.

#### Restarting the game

Use ![](manual-assets/refresh.svg) (**Start over**). Confirming returns to device preparation, stops sounds and discards pending actions from the previous game.

Restart has no keyboard shortcut.

## Game settings

Configure the game here before opening the Control Panel for live play. Everything is saved with the game and loaded automatically by the panel.

Open **Game settings** beside the selected game on **My games**.

### Teams

Enter team names and check them in the preview and Summary. On the game Display they appear only at the `Start game` screen, after devices and settings are prepared.

Empty fields use **Team A** and **Team B**.

### Appearance

#### Colours

Set four elements:

- **Team A colour** and **Team B colour** — team-related board elements.
- **Board background colour** — the Display's main background.
- **Dot colour** — dots used by the board's score displays.

Selections update the preview immediately. Click a colour field to open the picker.

#### Theme

Choose the visual theme for the whole Display. **Classic** is the default.

#### Logo

Choose your own logo under **Logo**. **Default** means Familiada's logo; **No logo** means no custom mark. The chosen logo appears at the opening; its ending appearance depends on the configured ending screen. Game settings previews unsaved edits, whereas the panel Summary uses saved settings.

`Restore defaults` in Appearance resets colours, theme and logo.

### Sound

Each game sound category has separate settings, including **Correct answer**, **Wrong answer**, **Intro**, **Round transition**, **Show outro music** and **Reveal**.

#### Sound variant

Choose a **variant** for each category:

- Presets such as **Classic**.
- **Custom** uploads an MP3, WAV or OGG; selecting it shows `Choose file`.

#### Custom audio file

Select `Choose file` and pick a file. Its name appears beside the category.

Remove it using ![](manual-assets/trash.svg). To return to a preset, choose a variant other than **Custom**.

Custom audio is saved in the cloud with the game and is available wherever you run it.

**Custom file limits:**

| Categories | Maximum duration |
| --- | --- |
| Buzzer press, correct/wrong answer, repeat, time limit, reveal | 5 seconds |
| Show intro music, round transition, final theme music | 30 seconds |
| Show outro music | 2 minutes |

`Finish game` plays the outro once the ending screen is already visible. Custom files and volume work the same way for every category.

#### Volume

Each category has a 0–100% volume slider. Saved levels load automatically in each game session.

#### Playback button

Select ![](manual-assets/play.svg) beside a category to preview it at the chosen volume. During playback it becomes ![](manual-assets/stop.svg), which stops the sound.

`Restore defaults` in Sound sets every variant to **Classic**, volumes to **100%**, and removes all custom audio locally and from the cloud. **This cannot be undone; files must be uploaded again.**

### Questions

#### Final

**Does the game include a final?** enables the final stage. When **off**, play ends after the main rounds. When **on**, final-question selection becomes available.

#### Round question mode

- **Random** shuffles the questions available for rounds when you enter the Control Panel Summary and shows the order. Questions selected manually for the final are skipped. Play then uses one question per round. **Reshuffle** changes the order before the game starts.
- **Order** uses only the questions selected and arranged in **Questions — Rounds**, in that exact order. No extra questions are added after the list runs out.

#### Final question mode

- **Random final** draws five questions from the rounds pool when entering the Summary, then removes them from the rounds queue so they cannot appear twice. With **Order**, the final is drawn from the manually selected list. **Reshuffle** returns the previous final questions to the end of the rounds queue and draws a new set before play. A full final needs at least five questions in that pool.
- **Manual final** requires exactly five questions in Game settings' **Questions — Final** tab. These questions are excluded from the rounds queue. Check the final and round lists in the panel Summary before starting.

#### Additional settings

Adjust the format's thresholds and pace without changing the rules.

- **Round multipliers** — comma-separated, such as `1,1,1,2,3`. Each round bank uses the corresponding multiplier, including classic doubling/tripling. Later rounds reuse the last multiplier.
- **Points threshold for the final** — a team's qualifying rounds score, classically 300. If neither team reaches it before questions run out, the rounds end naturally.
- **Final target (pts)** — the final points needed for the main prize, classically 200.
- **Game ending** — the screen shown after play:
- **Logo** — logo screen.
- **Points** — the winning team's final score.
- **Prize amount** — calculated monetary prize.
- **Prize multiplier (after final)** — if the target is missed, points earned throughout the game multiplied by this factor, classically ×3.
- **Main prize amount** — added to the prize when the final target is reached, classically 25,000.

The default values follow classic Familiada and suit most games.

**Warning:** Round and prize multipliers currently accept positive whole numbers. `0.5` is not supported.

#### Saving settings

Changes save when you select `Save all`. Editing a field does not auto-save: save before leaving.

#### Restore defaults

The page's `Restore defaults` button resets all game settings.

This is irreversible: after confirming accidentally, you must configure everything again, including re-uploading audio files.

The qualification threshold uses rounds points; the final target uses both players' final points. They are separate settings. With prize amounts enabled, reaching the final target includes the main prize; otherwise a lower prize is calculated from points and the multiplier. A game without a final shows points in this mode.
